Here's a question: when your parents go to work and earn money, they don't get to keep all of it. Some of it goes to the government. But why?

Because the government needs money to pay for things that everyone shares — schools, hospitals, roads, parks, the fire brigade, and much more. That money is called tax!
1
Income Tax: When adults earn money from their job, they pay a slice of it to the government. The more you earn, the bigger the slice — this is called a progressive tax, which means it's fairer for people who earn less.
2
VAT (Value Added Tax): When you buy most things in a shop — clothes, toys, electronics — 20% of the price goes to the government. It's already added in the price before you pay!
3
Where does it go? The government uses tax money to pay for the NHS, state schools, roads, police, the army, and benefits for people who need help. Everyone pays in, everyone benefits!

🧐 Quick Quiz!

🏭
The UK spends about £190 billion a year on the NHS — almost entirely funded by taxes. Every time you pay tax as an adult, you're helping keep hospitals running!
🧾
Children don't pay income tax — you only start when you earn above £12,570 a year (in 2025). But you do pay VAT whenever you buy something in a shop.
📚
The first income tax was introduced in Britain in 1799 by Prime Minister William Pitt to pay for the war against Napoleon. It was meant to be temporary!
