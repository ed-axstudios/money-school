Debt Detective

Not all borrowing is bad — learn when debt helps and when it hurts!

🤔
I'm Dexter the Debt Detective 🔎 — and debt is my speciality!

Here's the case: two friends both borrow money.
Mia borrows £200 to buy a new laptop so she can learn coding and get a job.
Tom borrows £200 on a credit card to buy sweets and video games, and forgets to pay it back.

One of them is in good debt. One is in bad debt. Can you work out which is which?
1
Good debt is borrowing that helps you earn MORE money or improve your life — like a mortgage to buy a house, or a student loan to get a degree that leads to a better job. The thing you bought is worth more than the debt.
2
Bad debt is borrowing to buy things that lose value quickly or disappear (like food, clothes, or gadgets) using expensive credit like a credit card or payday loan — and then struggling to pay it back.
3
Interest is the cost of borrowing. Whenever you borrow money, you pay back MORE than you borrowed. A credit card might charge 25% — so £100 borrowed for a year costs £125 to pay back. The longer you wait, the more it grows!

🧯 Good Debt or Bad Debt?

Tap each type of debt to reveal whether it's typically good or bad — and why!

🏠
A mortgage is usually "good debt" — you borrow to buy a house, which often goes up in value over time. After 25 years the house is yours, and worth far more than you paid.
🚧
Payday loans can charge over 1,000% interest per year — borrowing £100 for a month could cost £25 or more in fees. Always the most dangerous type of debt.
✨
The best strategy? Avoid bad debt completely. For good debt, always borrow as little as possible and pay it back as fast as you can to minimise the interest you pay.
